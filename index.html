01 / Vision

One framework.
Three coupled physical worlds.

Offshore turbines operate at the intersection of atmospheric turbulence, nonlinear ocean waves, rotating aerodynamics, structural motion, and wake interactions. NeWWave is designed to study those processes together rather than in isolation.

By connecting high-order CFD, aeroelastic turbine dynamics, and nonlinear wave modelling, the framework targets realistic, physics-rich simulations for offshore wind research and design.

03 / Capabilities

Built for multiscale offshore wind physics.

From the rotor scale to the wind-farm scale, NeWWave supports questions that depend on interactions across fluid, structural, and ocean domains.

01

Atmospheric turbulence

High-fidelity inflow, boundary-layer development, shear, turbulence spectra, and realistic wind conditions.

02

Actuator-line aerodynamics

Rotor forcing and wake generation with blade-resolved loading information from coupled turbine models.

03

Wake dynamics

Near- and far-wake evolution, wake recovery, tip-vortex breakdown, turbulent transport, and turbine-to-turbine interaction.

04

Aeroelastic response

Structural motion, rotor loads, controller effects, and coupled turbine response under turbulent offshore conditions.

05

Nonlinear wave fields

Irregular wave states and evolving free surfaces suitable for wind–wave interaction and offshore loading studies.

06

GPU-scale simulation

Designed around modern HPC workflows for computationally demanding, high-order simulations at research scale.

04 / Flow capability

Resolved flow fields.

NeWWave resolves the velocity field around wind turbines and through their wakes. It can capture wake development, recovery, and turbine-to-turbine interaction under turbulent offshore conditions.

05 / Research directions

Questions NeWWave is designed to answer.

The framework focuses on flow physics and engineering questions that emerge only when the atmosphere, rotor, structure, and ocean are treated as an interacting system.

01

How do waves modify turbine wakes?

Quantify the impact of wave-induced motion and marine boundary conditions on wake structure, transport, and recovery.

02

How do turbulent inflows change structural response?

Connect coherent atmospheric structures and wake events to time-varying rotor loads and turbine dynamics.

03

How do coupled effects scale across a wind farm?

Study the accumulation of wake–wave–structure interactions across multiple turbines and changing operating conditions.

04

Which physical mechanisms drive uncertainty?

Isolate the effects of turbulence, sea state, control, and structural motion on power, fatigue, and wake behaviour.

06 / Why it matters

Better physics for better offshore wind decisions.

NeWWave aims to reduce modelling gaps between idealized simulations and the coupled marine environment experienced by real offshore wind turbines.

07 / Researcher

About the researcher

Saroj Gautam

PhD Candidate · Mechanical & Mechatronics Engineering
Memorial University of Newfoundland

My research focuses on computational fluid dynamics, turbulence, and renewable-energy systems. I use high-fidelity numerical methods to study wind-turbine wakes, atmospheric boundary layers, offshore foundation flows, and the interaction between fluid flow and energy infrastructure.

NeWWave is part of this broader research direction. The framework brings NekRS, OpenFAST, and HOS together to investigate coupled wind–wave–turbine physics, including turbine loading, wake evolution, and wind-farm performance in realistic offshore environments.
